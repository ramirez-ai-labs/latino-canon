## RESEARCH PRIORITY TIERS

### **Tier 1: High-Likelihood Canon Candidates** (Research immediately)
- Early 1930s-50s foundational films with cultural significance
- Jorge Sanjinés films (Blood of the Condor, The Secret Nation) — Indigenous cinema pioneers
- Patricio Guzmán's Battle of Chile Parts II & III
- Luis Alcoriza films (multiple) — Mexican cinema master
- Arturo Ripstein films — Mexican auteur

### **Tier 2: Probable Candidates** (Research second batch)
- 1970s-80s political/dictatorship films
- Brazilian Cinema Novo second-wave (Macunaima, São Bernardo, Iracema)
- Cuban revolutionary cinema (Lucia, One Way or Another)
- Colombian documentary (Chircales, The Strategy of the Snail)

### **Tier 3: Evaluation Needed** (Batch 3)
- Pre-1945 cinema (authenticity, archival availability, cultural impact)
- Animated/genre films (Vampires in Havana, Torero)
- Haitian/Caribbean films (non-mainland Latin America)

### **Tier 4: Likely Exclusions** (Low priority, may skip)
- Non-Latino directors (Buñuel films already excluded)
- Films with ambiguous `about_community` (Tango, etc.)
- Heavily dependent on undocumented heritage claims

## NOTES FOR FUTURE RESEARCH

1. **Buñuel films are systematically excluded** — Spanish director, multiple films on IMDb list (The Young and the Damned, Nazarin, The Exterminating Angel, El). Mark all as excluded.

2. **Pre-1945 films** — Need verification of:
   - Actual Latino director/creator (heritage documentation)
   - Archival availability & streaming access
   - Cultural impact outside academia

3. **Caribbean/Non-Mainland** — Sugar Cane Alley (Martinique), Man on the Shore (Haiti), La boda (Cuba) — are these canonical for "Latin American" cinema or distinct regional cinemas?

4. **Bemberg & Aristarain** — Argentine directors with multiple works; worth deep dive into their complete filmographies

5. **Documentary films** — Several strong candidates (Battle of Chile parts, Chircales, Araya, Chile Obstinate Memory) — should these get equal weight with narrative films?
